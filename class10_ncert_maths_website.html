Chapter 1

Real Numbers

Euclid division lemma: a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.
For two positive integers: HCF × LCM = Product of the numbers.
Example: 135 = 45 × 3 + 0, so HCF(135,45) = 45.
Chapter 2

Polynomials

For ax² + bx + c, α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a.
Division algorithm: p(x) = g(x)q(x) + r(x).
Example: For 2x² + 5x + 3, sum of zeroes = −5/2 and product = 3/2.
Chapter 3

Pair of Linear Equations

a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → unique solution.
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → no solution.
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ → infinitely many solutions.
Example: x+y=7, x−y=1 ⇒ adding gives 2x=8 ⇒ x=4, y=3.
Chapter 4

Quadratic Equations

x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a
Discriminant D = b² − 4ac.
Example: x²−5x+6=0 ⇒ (x−2)(x−3)=0 ⇒ x=2 or 3.
Chapter 5

Arithmetic Progressions

aₙ = a + (n−1)d
Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d]
Also, Sₙ = n/2 (a + l), where l is the last term.
Example: 3, 7, 11,… has a=3,d=4. The 10th term = 3+9×4 = 39.
Chapter 6

Triangles

Similar triangles have corresponding sides proportional.
Pythagoras theorem: hypotenuse² = perpendicular² + base².
Example: Legs 6 cm and 8 cm ⇒ hypotenuse = √(36+64) = 10 cm.
Chapter 7

Coordinate Geometry

Distance = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Section formula (m:n): ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n))
Example: Distance between (0,0) and (3,4) = 5 units.
Chapter 8

Introduction to Trigonometry

sin θ = Perpendicular/Hypotenuse
cos θ = Base/Hypotenuse
tan θ = Perpendicular/Base
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ   |   1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
Example: If sin θ = 3/5 for an acute angle, then cos θ = 4/5.
Chapter 9

Applications of Trigonometry

tan θ = height / horizontal distance
Angle of elevation: line of sight is above the horizontal.
Angle of depression: line of sight is below the horizontal.
Example: If tan θ=1 and distance=20 m, height=20 m.
Chapter 10

Circles

Radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
Example: If PA and PB are tangents from P, then PA = PB.
Chapter 11

Areas Related to Circles

Circumference = 2πr
Area of circle = πr²
Area of sector = (θ/360°)πr²
Length of arc = (θ/360°)2πr
Example: r=7 cm ⇒ area = 49π cm² = 154 cm² using π=22/7.
Chapter 12

Surface Areas & Volumes

Cylinder: CSA=2πrh, Volume=πr²h
Cone: l=√(r²+h²), CSA=πrl, Volume=(1/3)πr²h
Sphere: SA=4πr², Volume=(4/3)πr³
Hemisphere: CSA=2πr², TSA=3πr², Volume=(2/3)πr³
Example: Cylinder with r=3,h=5 has volume = 45π cm³.
Chapter 13

Statistics

Mean (direct): x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ / Σfᵢ
Median = l + [(n/2 − cf)/f]h
Mode = l + [(f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)]h
Empirical relation: Mode ≈ 3 Median − 2 Mean.
Example: Data 2,4,6,8 has mean = 20/4 = 5.
Chapter 14

Probability

P(E) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of equally likely outcomes.
0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
P(not E) = 1 − P(E)
Example: Probability of getting an even number on a fair die = 3/6 = 1/2.

Algebra

(a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a−b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
a²−b² = (a−b)(a+b)

Geometry

Area of triangle = 1/2 × base × height
Pythagoras: c² = a²+b²
Circle area = πr²

Trigonometry

sin θ = P/H, cos θ = B/H, tan θ = P/B
sec θ = 1/cos θ, cosec θ = 1/sin θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ

📝 Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting values.
  • Keep units consistent in mensuration questions.
  • Show important steps instead of writing only the final answer.
  • For trigonometry, draw a labelled figure when a height/distance problem is given.
  • Check signs, square roots and units before submitting.
